\subsection{Background}

Gamification refers to the strategic application of game design principles, mechanics (such as points, levels,
and badges), and aesthetics (such as narrative and fantasy) into non-game educational environments 
(Christopoulos \& Mystakidis, 2023). By blending educational objectives with playful mechanics, this approach
aims to increase student engagement and motivate individuals toward their learning goals 
(Christopoulos \& Mystakidis, 2023).

This topic addresses the widespread problem of student disengagement, boredom, and lack of motivation in both 
traditional and online classrooms (Ruiz et al., 2024). In fully online environments. students often face challenges 
such as lack of in-person interaction and a lack of motivation to begin coursework, frequently resulting in students 
merely "lurking" without participating (Bai et al., 2022). Additionally, educational gamification addresses the 
"novelty effect" a phenomenon where students initially engage with gamified elements but gradually lose interest once 
they become familiar with the system's reward structures (Bai et al., 2022).

Addressing student disengagement is critical because motivation operates as the engine of learning, directly influencing
 the cognitive processes required for academic success (Christopoulos \& Mystakidis, 2023). Motivated students tend to 
 have clearer academic goals, assimilate information effectively, and achieve better grades (Jaramillo-Mediavilla et al., 2024).
 On the other hand, a lack of school commitment is directly related to poor academic performance, insufficient development 
 of socioemotional competencies, and low expectations regarding future performance (Ruiz et al., 2024).

The integration of gamification is currently being applied across all levels of the educational spectrum. In preschool and 
primary education, gamified interventions balance cooperation and competition to sharpen foundational academic abilities and 
problem-solving skills using virtual badges, story-driven adventures, and dynamic progression bars (Christopoulos \& Mystakidis, 2023).
In secondary education, gamification utilizes sophisticated. narrative-rich forms with branching storylines to hone critical thinking
and strategic planning (Christopoulos \& Mystakidis, 2023). In higher education and vocational training, interactive online courses 
simulate real-world professional challenges to cultivate practical skills (Christopoulos \& Mystakidis, 2023). Teachers across these 
levels utilize digital tools such as Kahoot, Quizizz, ClassDojo, and specialized learning management systems to incorporate
game elements into disciplines ranging from physical education to science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (Jaramillo-Mediavilla et al., 2024).

There is a vital need to study this topic because existing gamification research often lacks theoretical foundations, which leads to shortcomings 
in research designs and a partical view of its effectivenesss (Bai et al., 2022). Many studies measure school engagement in a fragmented way, focusing 
solely on motivation or participation, which highlights the need for a broader evaluation of behavioral, emotional, and cognitive dimensions (Ruiz et al., 2024).
Furthermore, continuous review is necessary to develop adaptive gamification frameworks that cater to individual student differences, overcome the 
short-term novelty effect, and ensure equitable access to educational technology (Christopoulos \& Mystakidis, 2023).  


\subsection{Problem Statement}

% Clearly describe the problem or issue that motivates this literature review.

Student engagement is critical for academic success, yet educational systems frequently struggle with student
disengagement, boredom, and a lack of motivation in both traditional and fully online learning environments.
To combat this, gamification has been widely adopted; however, evidence regarding its long-term effectiveness
in enhancing motivation remains inconsistent. A primary challenge is the "novelty effect" where students' initial
enthusiasm rapidly declines once they become familiar with the basic game mechanics, leading to a drop in 
sustained behavioral and affective engagement.

Furthermore, the current body of literature reveals significant theoretical and methodological gaps that hinder
the succsessful application of game-based learning. Gamification research often lacks comprehensive theoretical
foundations, resulting in poorly designed interventions that risk distracting from core learning objectives or 
over-emphasizing external rewards at the expense of intrinsic student motivation. Additionally, empirical studies
frequently measure school engagement in a fragmented manner, isolating single variables such as participation
or motivation rather than evaulating it holistically across cognitive, emotional, and behavioral dimensions.

Finally, the practical implementation of gamification is often hindered by a lack of teacher training, technological
limitations, and the challenge of seamlessly aligning game mechanics with specific curriculum goals. Because gamification
is sometimes applied as a superficial supplement rather than a thoughtfully integrated strategy, its true impact
on learning outcomes can be diluted. Therefore, there is a critical need for a comprehensive review to synthesize 
the disparate empirical evidence, address these theoretical and methodological shortcomings, and identify cohesive 
frameworks that can effectively sustain student motivation and improve academic performance across various educational contexts

\subsection{Objectives of the Review}

% State the objectives of the review article.

% \vspace{0.5cm}
% For example:

% \begin{enumerate}
%     \item To review existing research related to [topic].
%     \item To compare the approaches used by different researchers.
%     \item To analyse the findings and limitations of existing studies.
%     \item To identify research gaps and potential future research directions.

The primary aim of this review is to critically examine how gamification enhances 
educational outcomes and to identify strategies for overcoming current limitations 
in gamified learning environments. Specifically, this review seeks to achieve the 
following objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item To synthesize empirical evidence regarding the overall impact of 
    gamification mechanics on students' academic performance, cognitive engagement, 
    and task commitment.

    \item To analyze the theoretical frameworks (such as Self-Determination Theory) 
    underlying current gamification strategies and identify gaps where practical 
    applications lack theoretical grounding.

    \item To evaluate the effectiveness of deep, immersive game elements—such as 
    narrative and fantasy—in mitigating the "novelty effect" and sustaining long-term 
    intrinsic motivation compared to standard extrinsic rewards (e.g., points, badges, 
    and leaderboards).

    \item To assess the limitations of current "one-size-fits-all" gamification models 
    and explore the necessity of tailoring gamified interventions to diverse individual 
    learner profiles.
\end{enumerate}
