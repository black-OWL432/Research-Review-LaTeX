\subsection{Background}

Gamification, best described as integrating elements of game design into non-game contexts 
has emerged as a transformative research topic within modern education. Specifically, this 
research focuses on how gamification enhances learning experiences and  educational outcomes 
by utilizing mechanics such as points. badges, leaderboards, and immersive narratives. Recent 
studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of these techniques in boosting cognitive engagement 
and intrinsic motivation among students \parencite{Bai et al. 2022}

The primary problem this research addresses is the issue of student disengagement and the rapid
decline in motivation, particularly in the digital and online learning environments. Traditional
instructional models often struggle to sustain learner attention over time. Even when standard 
gamification is applied, students frequently experience a "novelty effect" where their initial 
excitement quickly fades, leading to a significant drop in participation and task completion as 
a course progresses \parencite{Bai et al. 2022}

Addresing this problem is critical because persistent disengagement directly corellates with 
poor academic performance, lower retention rates and high dropout rates in digital learning
environments. When students lose motivation, they fail to achieve deep learning, particularly
in complex or challenging subjects. By successfully keeping students engaged through interactive
and psychologically rewarding experiences, educators can foster a more resilient and self-motivated
learner mindset

Currently, gamification is being applied across a wide spectrum of educational settings and 
disciplines. It is extensively utilized in K-12 classrooms, higher education e-learning 
platforms, and corporate training modules. Several studies have investigated these approaches 
in specific domains such as science and STEM education, where interactive elements help bridge 
the gap between abstract, complex concepts and practical student understanding 
\parencite{(Bai et al., 2022; Kalogiannakis et al., 2021).}

Despite its widespread adoption, there is a pressing need to continuously study and review this topic. 
A significant portion of existing gamified educational systems are built arbitrarily without a 
foundation in established educational or psychological theories, such as Self-Determination 
Theory (Kalogiannakis et al., 2021). Furthermore, much of the current literature focuses narrowly 
on short-term academic performance rather than long-term behavioral changes and sustainability 
(Manzano-León et al., 2021). A comprehensive review is necessary to synthesize current findings, 
identify the limitations of "one-size-fits-all" gamification models, and guide instructional 
designers  toward creating theoretically sound, narrative-driven environments that truly sustain 
student motivation.


\subsection{Problem Statement}

% Clearly describe the problem or issue that motivates this literature review.

% \vspace{0.5cm}
% Explain:

Despite the widespread adoption of gamification in educational settings to enhance student 
engagement and academic performance, current implementations frequently fail to sustain 
long-term motivation and lack rigorous theoretical foundations. A primary issue is the 
"novelty effect" inherent in standard gamification models that rely solely on extrinsic 
rewards such as points, badges, and leaderboards. While these elements successfully 
capture initial student interest, this engagement rapidly diminishes over time, leading 
to significant drop-offs in participation and interaction as courses progress (Bai et al., 2022).

Furthermore, a critical gap exists in the design and evaluation of these gamified systems. 
The vast majority of current gamification applications are developed arbitrarily, without 
being grounded in established learning or psychological frameworks like Self-Determination 
Theory or Flow Theory (Kalogiannakis et al., 2021). Because game elements are rarely mapped 
to specific cognitive or behavioral needs, it is difficult to determine why certain mechanics 
succeed or fail. This lack of theoretical grounding, combined with a "one-size-fits-all" 
approach that ignores individual learner profiles, limits the scalability and effectiveness 
of gamified interventions (Kalogiannakis et al., 2021; Manzano-León et al., 2021).

Consequently, while gamification is broadly recognized as a valid learning strategy, existing 
research predominantly focuses on short-term academic outcomes (Manzano-León et al., 2021). 
The central problem remains: how can educators and instructional designers move beyond 
superficial extrinsic rewards to create theoretically grounded, highly immersive 
(e.g., narrative or fantasy-driven) gamified environments that intrinsically motivate 
students and sustain long-term educational engagement?

\subsection{Objectives of the Review}

% State the objectives of the review article.

% \vspace{0.5cm}
% For example:

% \begin{enumerate}
%     \item To review existing research related to [topic].
%     \item To compare the approaches used by different researchers.
%     \item To analyse the findings and limitations of existing studies.
%     \item To identify research gaps and potential future research directions.
% \end{enumerate}

The primary aim of this review is to critically examine how gamification enhances 
educational outcomes and to identify strategies for overcoming current limitations 
in gamified learning environments. Specifically, this review seeks to achieve the 
following objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item To synthesize empirical evidence regarding the overall impact of 
    gamification mechanics on students' academic performance, cognitive engagement, 
    and task commitment.

    \item To analyze the theoretical frameworks (such as Self-Determination Theory) 
    underlying current gamification strategies and identify gaps where practical 
    applications lack theoretical grounding.

    \item To evaluate the effectiveness of deep, immersive game elements—such as 
    narrative and fantasy—in mitigating the "novelty effect" and sustaining long-term 
    intrinsic motivation compared to standard extrinsic rewards (e.g., points, badges, 
    and leaderboards).

    \item To assess the limitations of current "one-size-fits-all" gamification models 
    and explore the necessity of tailoring gamified interventions to diverse individual 
    learner profiles.
\end{enumerate}
